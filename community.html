TCOSC Community

People learning, building, sharing, and growing together.

TCOSC is powered by students, developers, researchers, educators, professionals, mentors, and open-source contributors participating in Tanzania's computing ecosystem.

Participate

There are many ways to be part of the community.

Community participation is not limited to writing code. People contribute knowledge, experience, research, feedback, mentorship, and practical work in different ways.

Contributors

A community shaped by its contributors.

TCOSC brings together people at different stages of their computing journey. Everyone can contribute according to their knowledge, experience, interests, and available time.

Computing Students

Students can learn with peers, participate in practical work, contribute to open-source projects, and share their experiences.

Developers

Developers can contribute software, review code, improve engineering practices, and collaborate on projects.

Security Practitioners

Security-focused contributors can exchange knowledge, investigate technical problems, and support responsible security learning.

Researchers

Researchers can collaborate on investigations, experiments, technical publications, and reproducible research.

Educators

Educators and experienced practitioners can contribute knowledge, learning materials, guidance, and mentorship.

Open-Source Contributors

Contributors can improve code, documentation, tooling, infrastructure, and other shared community resources.

Mentorship

Experience becomes more valuable when it is shared.

TCOSC encourages experienced practitioners and peers to help others understand technical concepts, solve practical problems, improve projects, and navigate unfamiliar areas of computing.

Mentorship can happen through project work, technical discussions, code review, research, learning sessions, and direct guidance.

Community Principle

Learn from others. Help others learn.

A strong technical community grows when knowledge moves between people rather than remaining with individuals.

Community Conduct

Build a community where people can contribute well.

Technical communities depend on respectful collaboration, useful feedback, responsible contribution, and proper recognition of other people's work.

Respect

Treat other participants with professionalism and respect regardless of their experience level.

Constructive Feedback

Focus technical criticism on improving the work rather than attacking the person.

Attribution

Give appropriate credit to people whose ideas, code, research, or resources are used.

Responsible Contribution

Contribute in ways that protect other participants, projects, systems, and the wider community.
