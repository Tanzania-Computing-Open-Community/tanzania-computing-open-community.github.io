TCOSC Research

Investigate. Understand. Discover.

Explore technical questions through research, experimentation, evidence, and open sharing of knowledge across Tanzania's computing community.

Why Research

Good technical work begins with good questions.

Research helps us understand problems before deciding how to solve them. It allows ideas, technologies, systems, and assumptions to be examined using evidence rather than speculation.

Understand

Study Problems

Investigate how technologies, systems, users, and computing environments behave in real situations.

Question

Challenge Assumptions

Examine existing ideas, approaches, technologies, and claims instead of accepting them without evidence.

Evaluate

Test Approaches

Compare methods, implementations, technologies, or systems using appropriate research methods.

Share

Create Knowledge

Document findings so that other people can understand, evaluate, reproduce, or build upon the work.

Research Areas

Investigate problems across computing.

Research can cross traditional academic boundaries. Different technical areas can be combined when a research question requires it.

Computing Systems

Operating systems, distributed systems, infrastructure, performance, reliability, and system behavior.

Software Engineering

Software architecture, testing, maintainability, developer tools, software quality, and engineering practices.

Web Technologies

Web architecture, application behavior, performance, accessibility, protocols, and emerging technologies.

Networking

Protocols, network performance, infrastructure, distributed communication, reliability, and network behavior.

Cybersecurity

Vulnerability research, defensive security, application security, privacy, threat analysis, and security measurement.

Artificial Intelligence & Data

Machine learning, data processing, intelligent systems, model evaluation, data analysis, and responsible AI.

Human-Computer Interaction

Usability, accessibility, interfaces, user experience, and the interaction between people and computing systems.

Computing Education

Computing education, learning technologies, technical skill development, educational systems, and learning practices.

Research Lifecycle

Move from a question to evidence.

Research requires a structured process for transforming a technical question into evidence-based understanding.

01

Question

Identify a meaningful technical question that needs to be understood.

02

Background

Study existing knowledge, previous work, and the context surrounding the question.

03

Problem

Define the specific problem, objectives, scope, assumptions, and research questions.

04

Method

Select appropriate methods for collecting and evaluating evidence.

05

Experiment

Conduct the investigation, collect observations, measurements, or other relevant evidence.

06

Analysis

Examine the evidence and determine what it supports, contradicts, or leaves unresolved.

07

Conclusion

Explain what was learned, including limitations, implications, and remaining questions.

08

Share

Communicate the research and make appropriate supporting material available to others.

Research Projects

A place for real investigations.

This area is intended for actual research carried out through the TCOSC ecosystem. Each research project should be represented by its real question, methodology, evidence, findings, and supporting materials.

Research Space

Research projects will grow from real investigations.

As members conduct research, this space can present the problem being investigated, the methods used, research status, outputs, and links to supporting technical material.

No research project is presented here until there is real work behind it.

Final-Year Research

Let strong student research become lasting knowledge.

Final-year research often contains valuable technical investigation that disappears after academic assessment. TCOSC can provide a pathway for suitable research to remain accessible beyond the original submission.

Research can be shared through reports, experiments, prototypes, software, datasets, papers, and other appropriate outputs.

Research Outputs

A research submission can become a foundation for further work.

Documentation, evidence, code, and findings can help other researchers continue the investigation.

Open Research

Share enough for others to understand the work.

Open research is not simply publishing a final document. Where appropriate, research should make its methods, evidence, supporting material, and limitations visible.

Research Questions

Clearly state what the investigation is attempting to understand or determine.

Methodology

Document how the investigation was designed and how evidence was collected.

Data & Evidence

Share appropriate supporting data, observations, measurements, or other evidence.

Source Code

Where software supports the research, source code can make the implementation available for examination.

Results

Present findings clearly and distinguish evidence from interpretation.

Limitations

Explain constraints, uncertainty, weaknesses, and questions that remain unresolved.

Research Outputs

Research can produce more than papers.

Different investigations require different forms of technical output. The useful output depends on the research question and methodology.

Research Papers

Formal communication of research questions, methods, findings, and conclusions.

Technical Reports

Detailed documentation of technical investigations and their results.

Datasets

Structured research data that can support analysis, evaluation, or further investigation.

Software

Implementations, tools, or systems created to support or demonstrate research.

Prototypes

Experimental implementations used to investigate technical ideas or possible solutions.

Replication Studies

Independent attempts to reproduce or evaluate previous findings and determine whether they hold under new conditions.

Experiments

Controlled technical investigations designed to produce measurable or observable evidence.

Research Notes

Technical observations and intermediate findings that can help communicate the development of an investigation.

Research Collaboration

Different skills strengthen research.

Research can involve people with different technical and academic strengths working together around the same question.

Students

Develop research questions, conduct investigations, build technical artifacts, and document findings.

Researchers

Contribute research methods, technical knowledge, investigation experience, and critical analysis.

Developers

Build software, prototypes, experiments, and technical infrastructure supporting research.

Technical Practitioners

Bring practical experience and real-world technical context to research problems.

Reviewers

Examine methods, assumptions, implementation details, evidence, and conclusions.

Mentors

Help researchers improve the clarity, rigor, and practical relevance of their investigations.

Research Quality

Evidence matters more than impressive claims.

TCOSC research should aim for technical honesty, clear methodology, responsible investigation, and conclusions that are supported by the available evidence.

Evidence

Claims should be supported by observations, measurements, data, analysis, or appropriate sources.

Reproducibility

Research should document enough of its process for others to understand or reproduce the work where practical.

Transparency

Methods, assumptions, limitations, and uncertainty should be communicated honestly.

Responsible Research

Research should consider ethical, legal, privacy, security, and safety implications.

Technical Rigor

Research methods should be appropriate to the question being investigated.

Attribution

Existing work, sources, datasets, software, and contributors should receive appropriate recognition.

Research & Projects

Research can become practical technology.

An investigation can produce a prototype, tool, implementation, dataset, or software project.

At the same time, real software can reveal new technical questions that deserve further investigation.
